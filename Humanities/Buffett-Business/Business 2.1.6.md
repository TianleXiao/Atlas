## HR Strategies For Dealing With Resistance To Change
![[8c42563db1c819021d7db7dddee18756.jpg]]

Change management refers to processes and techniques used to plan, implement and evaluate changes in business operations. Business organizations are dynamic in nature and are always subject to the forces of change, such as globalization, technological advancements, climate change, the Covid-19 pandemic, etc..

One of the most difficult tasks for any leader is to manage change. Trying to force change is likely to cause problems, as people can be resistant to change and uncooperative if change is simply made compulsory.

Yet, without change, firms can become complacent, uncompetitive or even irrelevant.

Strategies for dealing with resistance to change include the following six methods, as advocated by John Paul Kotter and Leonard Schlesinger of Harvard Business School (1979):

- Education and communication
- Participation and involvement
- Facilitation and support
- Negotiation and agreement
- Manipulation and co-option
- Explicit and implicit coercion

## HR Strategies For Dealing With Resistance 3 and 4
![[7024a63a0d2540731be79c52e7b46893.jpg]]

3. Facilitation and support

This approach to change management can come in numerous forms, such as retraining of staff to enable them to cope with the new changes, or counselling workers to deal with their fears and anxieties, thereby averting potential resistance to change and helping people to accept change instead.

4. Negotiation and agreement

This is the 'carrot' approach as it involves using incentives as a motivational tool to overcome resistance to change among employees. Managers offer benefits such as amended employment contracts or early retirement packages to encourage acceptance of new changes. Negotiations may lead to improved outcomes, different from the initial plans.

## HR Strategies For Dealing With Resistance 5 and 6
![[fecb282b917a5fb3b711de33547f03b8.jpg]]

5. Manipulation and co-option

This strategy involves including a representative of the resistant group in the change process to communicate the benefits of change. However, the real aim is to change the representative's perspective. Despite symbolic roles, these representatives, like union leaders, don't influence management's determination for change. While this approach can be perceived as unethical, it may backfire if the resisting individuals realize the true intentions of management.

6. Explicit and implicit coercion

The 'stick' approach to managing resistance to change involves using coercive tactics as a last resort, such as threats of disciplinary action, dismissals, job losses, or lack of promotions, to compel employees to accept change even if they disagree. Coercion forces employees to accept change against their will. Over time, they may come to accept change, especially if it proves successful, but coercion is often done implicitly due to employment legislation protecting workers.
