## Formative Appraisal
![[3153f3ea0409dd4f4ba4903d91f1e797.jpg]]
Formative appraisal

Formative appraisal is a planned (formative) and ongoing process in which appraisal evidence is used by employees to inform them about what to do to improve their work practices.

The goals of formative appraisal include:
- Monitor the performance of employees' learning
- Help employees to identify their strengths and weaknesses (areas that need developing)
- Help managers to recognize areas where staff are struggling so that any problems can be addressed promptly

## Summative Appraisal
![[3d3c6fa3cbad225dc3a5fc8fd5ff7b67.jpg]]
Summative appraisal

"When the cook tastes the soup, that's formative; when the guests taste the soup, that's summative." — Robert E. Stake (b. 1927), Professor Emeritus of Education at the University of Illinois

A summative appraisal is a written description of an employee's performance at work, with recommendations for improvement.

The goals of summative appraisal include:
- Evaluate the performance or contribution of workers by comparing this with a predetermined standard or benchmark.

## 360-Degree Appraisal
![[0dff4ccb1d7466f34b30c350001aee49.jpg]]
360-degree appraisal

A 360-degree appraisal gathers feedback on an employee's performance from a range of people who work with them — subordinates, peers, line managers and, in some schemes, external stakeholders such as customers and suppliers — rather than relying on the line manager alone.

Typical benefits:
- Provides a fuller, more balanced picture of an employee's behaviour and contribution.
- Reduces the bias that comes from a single appraiser.
- Encourages teamwork, as peers are given a formal voice in assessing one another.

Typical drawbacks:
- Time-consuming and administratively heavy for the business.
- Feedback can be distorted by personal relationships or by a desire not to offend colleagues.
- Employees may distrust its anonymity and, if feedback is not handled carefully, it can damage working relationships.

## Structured Interview Questions
![[20261123_133239.jpg]]
Structured interviews

Behavioural-based questions are used to assess a candidate's behavioural pattern and initiative. Such questions typically start off as "Explain an example of when you..." or "Tell me about..."

Situational-based questions are used to assess an applicant's judgemental ability. The interview starts with a hypothetical scenario and asks the interviewee for a response.

These interviews have:
- some flexibility, such as asking further questions based on what the respondent says in the interview.
- however, if the same fundamental questions are asked to all candidates, interview bias can be reduced.
