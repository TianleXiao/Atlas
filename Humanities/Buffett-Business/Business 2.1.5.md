5. The gig economy (disadvantages)
- A gig economy reduces the need for full-time workers in the traditional economy, possibly limiting people's potential for career development and advancement.
- Gig workers do not have fixed employment contracts, so may lack job security and a stable income. There is pressure for gig workers to continually look for the next job. At times, gig workers are not even guaranteed to earn the national minimum wage.
- As self-employed people, gig workers have setup costs of their own, such as the need to purchase their own bicycles or motor vehicles, high-specs smartphones for delivery-related services.
- The lack of a full-time employment contract also means gig workers may miss out on employee rights and benefits, such as paid sick leave, holiday/redundancy pay and maternity leave.


### Reasons for Resistance to Change
![[Pasted image 20261020093601.png]]

"It is easier to change mountains and rivers than to alter one's character" - Chinese Proverb

In general, change creates uncertainty for people so there is often resistance to change. There are four interrelated reasons for resistance to change in the workplace:
- 1. Self-interest
- 2. Low tolerance
- 3. Misinformation and misunderstanding
- 4. Interpretations of circumstances

1. Self-interest

Employees are often more interested in and/or worried about the implications of change for themselves, rather than the possible benefits to the organization as a whole. Hence, there is resistance to change as they may feel it is unnecessary and/or requires too much effort on their part.

The reason for resistance to change may also occur from a perceived threat to a person's job security, status/rank, and financial position. It is human nature to pursue self-interest.


### Reasons for Resistance to Change (Continued)
![[Pasted image 20261020093617.png]]

2. Low tolerance

People tend to like stability and normality (or inertia) in their personal and professional lives. Change often entails new policies and procedures or new ways of doing things in the workplace. However, humans need an element of security and predictability in their lives. Hence, people often have a low tolerance of change or are fearful of it or simply worry that they cannot adapt to it.

3. Misinformation and misunderstandings

If the purpose and potential benefits of change are not clearly and effectively communicated to the workforce, a lack of understanding arises, which creates opposition to change. Misperceptions may be widespread because of informal communications (such as gossip) and workers may feel that change is unnecessary, especially if the business is not in a crisis or faced with adverse conditions.


### Reasons for Resistance to Change (Continued)
![[Pasted image 20261020093633.png]]

4. Interpretation of circumstances (different assessment of the situation)

Different people can have different interpretations or views of a given situation. Employees and employers may disagree on the rationale for and the benefits of change. Workers may believe that senior managers do not know what they are doing or why they are doing it and they may feel that the business is not acting in their best interest. Indeed, they may feel that they have better solutions and would change things in more suitable ways.

Note: This reason for the resistance to change is different from "self-interest" because the restraining force in this case is based on competing interpretations or assessments of what is best for the business and its stakeholders.
