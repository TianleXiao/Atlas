## Contribution Pricing Vs Cost-Plus Pricing (HL)

| | Cost-plus pricing (SL and HL) | Contribution pricing (HL only) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focus | Focuses on a firm's total costs (the sum of fixed and variable costs) when setting the price of a product | Focuses on the contribution each sale makes towards covering a firm's fixed costs in order to generate a profit |
| Calculation | Add all costs associated with producing or delivering the product, then add a desired profit to determine the price. | Calculate the variable costs of producing the product, then subtract these from the selling price to find the contribution per unit. |
| Purpose (use) | To ensure that a firm covers all its costs and makes a predetermined profit. | To ensure that each sale contributes enough to cover variable costs and towards covering fixed costs so as to generate a profit. |

## Contribution Pricing Advs (HL)
- Contribution analysis enables managers to determine which products from the firm's product portfolio generate the highest (and the least) amount of total contribution. Products with the highest total contribution are prioritized as these contribute the most towards paying the firm's fixed costs. Products that contribute the least amount may be discontinued.
- It is straightforward to calculate, so long as managers establish the variable cost per unit, fixed costs, and a selling price.
- Contribution pricing enables managers to know how much profit the business will earn on each unit sold after the firm has reached its break-even level of sales.
- It is a useful pricing method to decide on the price to charge customers for a special or additional order.
