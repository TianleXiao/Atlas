## Centralization And Decentralization
![[ec11185d32627d6d518f500caf468a1e.jpg]]

- In a centralized structure, decision-making is made by senior management team without consultation with other members of the organization.
- In a decentralized structures, decision-making authority and responsibility is shared with others.
  - Departments or regional offices might be empowered to make decisions on behalf of the overall organization.
  - However, key strategic decisions are still concentrated in the hands of the CEO and the board of directors.



---

## Centralization
![[a2d974c5605dce2ffa6f4d3fe55184ab.jpg]]

Advantages

- Avoid decision-making: no need to consult staffs for opinions
- Better control: managers can have a better overview and tighter control of what is happening in their organisations.
- Better sense of direction: as there are fewer decision-makers, consistency in approach is more likely to achieved.
- Efficiency: tasks are less likely to be repeated by different people or departments.

Disadvantages

- Added pressure for senior staff: decision-makers do not delegate authority so could face huge pressures from the extra workload.
- Inflexibility: workers have very limited autonomy (simply follow the orders).
- Possible delays in decision-making
- Demotivation: workers may feel less valued as they lack opportunities to make decisions.

## Organisational Chart By Region
![[0ff01b8b397d7302e111da30da5ed422.jpg]]

ORGANISATIONAL CHART BY REGION

- Director General (IBO)
  - The Americas
  - Africa, Europe and the Middle East
  - Asia-Pacific

Organization by region means structuring a workforce according to different geographical areas based on where the firm's operations are.

- Multinational companies are often organized by geographical region.
- This allows the business to be more aware of and responsive to local cultural differences and consumer needs.
- Such organizational structures allow regional managers to have better overall control over staffing and training issues.
